Afternoon

The groom is readied and the sehra is tied. Lovekesh’s family gathers to bless him and take far too many photographs, before the drums start.

3:00pm

Baraat

The procession sets off. Dhol, dancing, and the slowest hundred metres of the day. The bride’s side waits at the gate with a few tricks of their own.

The ceremony

The exchange of garlands. Expect Lovekesh’s friends to lift him out of reach, and Aakanksha’s side to have planned for it.

What to wear

Dress for the colours of the days

These are suggestions, not rules. Wear what makes you feel wonderful.

Cocktail

Evening elegance. Cocktail dresses, Indo-western, a well-cut jacket.

Haldi & Mehendi

Light and easy ethnic wear in pastels and florals. Turmeric does not come out, so nothing precious.

Sangeet

Festive and a little glamorous. Embroidery, shimmer, things that move when you dance.

Wedding day

Traditional Indian formal. Sarees, lehengas, sherwanis, bandhgalas. Please leave bridal red for the bride.

January in Rajasthan is sunny by day and genuinely cold once the sun sets, often around 7°C. Bring a shawl or a jacket for the evenings, and shoes that can manage lawns.

The venue

Ananta Spa & ResortAjabgarh, Rajasthan

Thirty acres of lawns and villas in a valley of the Aravalli foothills, about two hours’ drive from Jaipur, with the ramparts of Ajabgarh Fort on the ridge above and Bhangarh a short drive away. All three days take place within the resort.

Between celebrations there’s a long pool deck, the Tattva spa, and mustard fields to walk through at golden hour.
